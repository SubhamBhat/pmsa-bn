\section{Query 1: Why Pseudo-Count Shrinkage When the Mask Already Shrinks Proportionately with Image Dimensions?}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{The Core Distinction: Geometric Resizing vs. Statistical Shrinkage}
The terminology ``shrinkage'' has two distinct meanings in computer vision and statistics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Geometric / Spatial Shrinkage (Downsampling):} The reduction of spatial grid resolution as feature maps pass through pooling and strided convolution layers (e.g., $256 \times 256 \to 128 \times 128 \to \dots \to 8 \times 8$).
    \item \textbf{Statistical Shrinkage (James-Stein / Bayesian Shrinkage):} Regularizing a high-variance sample estimator ($\hat{\mu}_k, \hat{\sigma}^2_k$) by shrinking it toward an informative prior ($\mu_k^{\mathrm{src}}, \sigma_k^{2,\mathrm{src}}$) when the effective sample size $N_k$ is critically small.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=Key Insight]
Statistical shrinkage is not an alternative to spatial downsampling; \textbf{it is mathematically required precisely because spatial downsampling occurs.}
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{The Small-Sample Catastrophe at Deep Bottleneck Layers}
Consider a high-resolution input image ($256 \times 256 = 65{,}536$ pixels) containing a fine anatomical structure (such as an optic cup or a diminutive polyp) occupying $0.2\%$ of the image area ($\approx 130$ pixels):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shallow Layers ($256 \times 256$ or $128 \times 128$):} The structure covers over 100 spatial feature positions. The sample mean and sample variance can be estimated with reasonable empirical stability.
    \item \textbf{Deep Bottleneck Layers ($8 \times 8 = 64$ total positions):} Because the mask downsamples proportionately with the feature map resolution, the expected spatial footprint at the bottleneck becomes:
    \begin{equation}
        N_k = 64 \times 0.002 \approx \mathbf{0.13 \text{ to } 1 \text{ feature cell}}.
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

When $N_k \le 1$ at single-image test-time adaptation ($B=1$), standard empirical estimation collapses:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unbiased Sample Variance Divergence / Division by Zero:}
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{\sigma}^2_k = \frac{1}{N_k - 1} \sum_{i,j} w_k(i,j)\left(Z(i,j) - \hat{\mu}_k\right)^2
    \end{equation}
    When $N_k = 1$, the denominator $N_k - 1 = 0$, rendering the variance \textbf{undefined} (division by zero). If estimated with $N_k$ in the denominator, the variance is trivially $\hat{\sigma}^2_k = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Estimator Variance Explosion:} Even when $N_k = 2$ or $3$, the variance of the sample variance estimator is given by:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{Var}(\hat{\sigma}^2_k) = \frac{2\sigma^4}{N_k - 1} \xrightarrow{N_k \to 1} \infty.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Batch Normalization Activation Blow-Up:} Standard BN normalizes activations via:
    \begin{equation}
        y = \gamma \frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}} + \beta.
    \end{equation}
    If $\sigma^2 \approx 0$, features are amplified by $\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10^{-5}}} \approx 316\times$, causing severe numerical instability and NaN values. In our empirical ablation on the optic cup, omitting shrinkage caused Dice to collapse from \textbf{0.581 to 0.094}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Conjugate Bayesian Solution}
PMSA-BN regularizes empirical moments via Normal-Inverse-Gamma conjugate pseudo-counts $\kappa_\mu = 8$ and $\kappa_\sigma = 16$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mu}_k = \frac{N_k \hat{\mu}_k + \kappa_\mu \mu_k^{\mathrm{src}}}{N_k + \kappa_\mu}, \qquad 
    \tilde{\sigma}^2_k = \frac{(N_k - 1)_+ \hat{\sigma}^2_k + \kappa_\sigma \sigma_k^{2,\mathrm{src}}}{(N_k - 1)_+ + \kappa_\sigma}.
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dominant Classes ($N_k \gg \kappa$, e.g., Background):} $\frac{N_k}{N_k + \kappa} \to 1$, fully adapting to the target distribution.
    \item \textbf{Vanishing Structures ($N_k \le 1 \ll \kappa$, Bottleneck):} Target weight smoothly drops to $0$, and the estimator safely reverts to the stable source prior $\sigma_k^{2,\mathrm{src}}$, eliminating variance collapse.
\end{itemize}

\section{Query 2: Why Is the Variance Formula Summing Over $k$ (Classes vs. Channels)?}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Clarification of Indices: $k$ vs. $c$}
The index $k$ in Eq.~(1) denotes \textbf{semantic anatomical classes}, NOT feature channels:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$ indexes disjoint semantic regions (e.g., $k=1$: Background, $k=2$: Optic Disc Rim, $k=3$: Optic Cup).
    \item $c \in \{1, 2, \dots, C\}$ indexes the feature channels.
\end{itemize}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=Important Architectural Detail]
Channels are \textbf{never mixed or summed over} in Eq.~(1). The formula is evaluated \textbf{strictly independently for each individual channel $c$}. The subscript $c$ was suppressed in the manuscript solely to avoid notation clutter.
\end{tcolorbox}

With explicit channel indices restored, the equation reads:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_c^2 = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \sigma_{c,k}^2 + \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \left(\mu_{c,k} - \mu_c\right)^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Mathematical Derivation: The Law of Total Variance}
Let random variable $X$ denote the activation values within channel $c$, and let discrete random variable $Y \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ denote the class assignment of each spatial position with prior probability $P(Y = k) = \pi_k$.

By the \textbf{Law of Total Variance} (also known as \emph{Eve's Law} or the \emph{ANOVA variance decomposition}):
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Var}(X \mid Y)\right] + \mathrm{Var}\left(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]\right).
\end{equation}

Evaluating both terms yields:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Expected Conditional Variance (Within-Class Variance):}
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Var}(X \mid Y)\right] = \sum_{k=1}^K P(Y=k) \mathrm{Var}(X \mid Y=k) = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \sigma_{c,k}^2.
    \end{equation}
    This measures the average internal activation spread within each individual anatomical tissue.
    \item \textbf{Variance of Conditional Expectation (Between-Class Variance):}
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{Var}\left(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]\right) &= \sum_{k=1}^K P(Y=k)\left(\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y=k] - \mathbb{E}[X]\right)^2 \nonumber \\
        &= \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \left(\mu_{c,k} - \mu_c\right)^2.
    \end{align}
    This measures the activation variance arising from the contrast difference between distinct anatomical classes.
\end{enumerate}

Adding both terms yields the total pooled variance $\sigma_c^2$ of channel $c$ across all spatial locations.

\subsection{Why This Decomposition Is Fundamental to PMSA-BN}
Traditional AdaBN computes pooled sample variance $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{AdaBN}}$ across all pixels. If the target patient has an unusually small tumor ($\pi_{\mathrm{tumor}} \downarrow$), the between-class variance term $\sum_k \pi_k (\mu_k - \mu)^2$ alters $\sigma^2$ even when the physical appearance and scanner contrast of both tissues ($\mu_k, \sigma_k^2$) have not changed at all. 

By decomposing the variance into class-conditional moments, PMSA-BN recombines $(\mu_k, \sigma_k^2)$ with the \textbf{source priors} $\pi_k^{\mathrm{src}}$, provably decoupling scanner appearance shift from inter-patient anatomical size variations (Proposition~1).
