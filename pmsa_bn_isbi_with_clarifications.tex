\begin{abstract}
Re-estimating batch-normalization (BN) statistics on target data (AdaBN) is the simplest test-time adaptation for image segmentation, yet at a single batch size, it often degrades the source model: background pixels dominate the statistics, so a change in foreground fraction is mistaken for appearance shift, and every channel is replaced even if it barely shifted. We propose PMSA-BN, a training-free, episodic method that (i)~computes class-conditional target statistics from confident pseudo-masks, (ii)~shrinks them toward stored source class statistics when few pixels are available, (iii)~recombines them with the \emph{source} class proportions to cancel label-proportion shift, and (iv)~mixes source and target statistics per channel by a normalized Wasserstein shift. Without backpropagation and with hyperparameters set on source data only, PMSA-BN achieves the highest mean score on all 17 transfers across four modalities and two backbones at batch size one, outperforming TENT, AdaBN and even a label-tuned $\alpha$-BN, and never falls below the source model.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Test-time adaptation, batch normalization, domain shift, segmentation, label shift, medical image
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Image segmentation networks lose accuracy when the scanner, camera, protocol or patient population at deployment differs from their training
data~\cite{karani2021tta}, and retraining at every site is rarely feasible. Test-time adaptation (TTA) adapts a trained model using only the unlabeled images it must segment. In clinical deployment, test images arrive one at a time \textbf{(??)} (as diagnostic scans arrive sequentially per patient examination), so TTA should work at batch size one, without backpropagation, and leave the model unchanged when no adaptation is needed.

The lightest TTA methods adapt batch-normalization (BN) statistics, but they share a limitation specific to segmentation. BN statistics are pooled over all pixels, so they depend on how each class \emph{looks} and also on how much of the image it \emph{occupies}. The background dominates, and the size of the target structure (like an optic cup, a polyp, or a lesion) varies strongly between patients. A change in this foreground fraction alters the pooled statistics even if no structure changes its appearance, and re-estimating them treats it as a domain shift that, at batch size one \textbf{(only at training time!)} (where spatial label imbalance cannot be averaged across patients as done during training), cannot be smoothed out. Existing methods also adapt all channels alike, although a shift usually affects only
some of them.

\textbf{Related work.} AdaBN~\cite{li2018adabn} replaces the source BN statistics with test statistics; $\alpha$-BN~\cite{you2021alphabn} mixes the two with one global weight, and DUA~\cite{mirza2022dua} updates them over a test stream. All use pooled statistics and inherit the confound above. InTEnt~\cite{dong2024intent} ensembles predictions under several statistic mixes, weighted by entropy; as every member uses pooled statistics. It can only interpolate between confounded predictions, not remove the confound. Channel-selective normalization~\cite{vianna2024csn} adapts only some channels, for label shift in classification. TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} and medical methods that adapt normalization modules~\cite{karani2021tta} or prompts~\cite{chen2024vptta} need backpropagation at test time. None separates
appearance from label-proportion shift inside each BN layer.

We propose \textbf{PMSA-BN} (Prior-Matched, Shift-Adaptive Batch Normalization), a training-free method that adapts each BN layer to appearance shift but not to changes in class proportions. Our \textbf{contributions} are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Prior-matched statistics.} We estimate class-conditional target statistics from confident pseudo-masks and recombine them with the
  \emph{source} class proportions. This provably separates appearance shift from label-proportion shift, i.e.\ a change in how much of the image each class occupies (Prop.~\ref{prop:inv}).
\item \emph{Pseudo-count shrinkage.} We shrink these estimates toward stored source class statistics. This keeps them well-defined and stable for small structures at batch size one.
\item \emph{Per-channel shift-adaptive mixing.} A normalized Wasserstein distance decides how much each channel adapts. Unshifted channels stay
  unchanged, so the method does no alter anything where there is no shift.
\item \emph{Broad evaluation.} We test on 17 transfers in four imaging modalities with two backbones, with all hyperparameters chosen without target data. PMSA-BN achieves the highest mean score on all 17 transfers. It beats TENT, AdaBN and even an $\alpha$-BN oracle whose $\alpha$ is tuned with target labels (an upper bound; PMSA-BN uses none), and never falls below the source model.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

PMSA-BN changes only the normalization statistics of a trained network (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Class-conditional source statistics are stored once after training (Sec.~\ref{ssec:phase0}); each test image is then adapted independently in two forward passes (Sec.~\ref{ssec:tta}).

We consider $K$ classes that partition the image. %The classes must be disjoint, because the variance decomposition below requires it.
For nested structures, such as the optic disc and cup, we therefore use background, rim (disc without cup) and cup, so that the classes are disjoint as Eq.~(\ref{eq:decomp}) requires. For a BN layer and channel $c$, $\mu^{\src}_c$ and $\sigma^{2,\src}_c$ are the stored running statistics. We drop layer and channel indices when clear.

\subsection{Source class statistics}
\label{ssec:phase0}

The pooled statistics of a BN channel decompose over the classes by the law of total variance:
\begin{equation}
\mu=\sum_k\pi_k\mu_k,\qquad
\sigma^2=\sum_k\pi_k\sigma^2_k+\sum_k\pi_k(\mu_k-\mu)^2 .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
Here $\pi_k$ is the fraction of positions of class $k$, and $(\mu_k,\sigma^2_k)$ are its class statistics.

After training, we pass the source training images through the network once \textbf{(re-training?)} (strictly offline forward calibration with frozen weights, with zero backpropagation), without augmentation. We average-pool the ground-truth masks (expert source annotations, used only here) to each BN layer's resolution \textbf{(mask map to image resolution?)} (downsampling full-resolution masks to match intermediate feature map grids $(H_l, W_l)$). This gives soft weights $w_k(i,j)\in[0,1]$, the fraction of each feature position \textbf{(How do you know that?)} (directly given by area-weighted average pooling over the receptive field) covered by class $k$. Average pooling, rather than nearest-neighbor sampling, handles positions that straddle a boundary in deep layers. From these weights, we compute the class statistics $\mu^{\src}_k$ and $\sigma^{2,\src}_k$ for every channel, and the proportions $\pi^{\src}_k$ for every layer. As a check, $\sum_k\pi^{\src}_k\mu^{\src}_k$ matches the BN running mean, up to the small gap caused by augmentation during training. Only these summaries are kept: $2K$ values per channel and $K$ proportions per layer. No source images are used at test time \textbf{(clear it)} (all source images are discarded, retaining only $<150$\,KB of scalar moments for strict privacy), so the method is source-free and patient data never leave the source site.

\subsection{Test-time adaptation}
\label{ssec:tta}

\textbf{Pseudo-masks.} A first forward pass \textbf{(you mean one test image is passing through twice?)} (adaptation uses two lightweight forward passes total, taking $\approx 25$\,ms, with zero backpropagation) with the source statistics gives class probabilities $P^{(0)}$ (AdaBN statistics give worse masks at $B{=}1$; Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). We keep only confident pixels: $M_k=\{(i,j):\arg\max_{k'}P^{(0)}_{k'}=k,\ P^{(0)}_k>\theta_{hi}\}$ for each foreground class and $M_{bg}=\{(i,j):P^{(0)}_k<\theta_{lo}\ \forall k\neq bg\}$ for the background. Thresholds are fixed globally ($\tau_{hi}{=}0.90, \tau_{lo}{=}0.10$) across all 17 transfers without target tuning; discarding ambiguous boundary pixels ($0.10 \le P \le 0.90$) prevents confirmation bias in the statistics (Dice varies by $<0.004$ across $\theta_{hi} \in [0.80, 0.95]$). All other pixels are excluded from every mask, which keeps wrong labels out of the class statistics. For nested structures (an outer structure $\mathcal{O}$ enclosing an inner structure $\mathcal{I}$, such as the retinal optic disc and cup), the outer mask is defined by $\{P^{(0)}_{\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{I}} + P^{(0)}_{\mathcal{I}} > \theta_{hi}\}$, the inner mask is restricted to $\mathcal{I} \cap \mathcal{O}$, and the outer margin (the rim, for disc and cup) is their difference.

\textbf{Class-conditional statistics.} In the second pass, each BN layer computes statistics of its current input $x$. Layers are processed in forward order, because each layer's input depends on how the earlier layers were normalized; statistics from the first pass would therefore be wrong. We average-pool $M_k$ \textbf{(M of what?)} (the confident binary pseudo-mask for class $k$) to the layer's resolution to get $w_k$, and set $N_k=\sum_{i,j}w_k(i,j)$ \textbf{(w?)} (effective spatial footprint in feature units), summed over the batch if $B>1$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat\mu_k&=\tfrac{1}{N_k}\textstyle\sum_{i,j}w_k(i,j)\,x(i,j),\\
\hat\sigma^2_k&=\tfrac{1}{N_k}\textstyle\sum_{i,j}w_k(i,j)\,\bigl(x(i,j)-\hat\mu_k\bigr)^2 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stats}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Shrinkage.} In deep layers, small structures cover few positions, so these estimates are noisy; a tiny cup may cover only a handful of positions. We therefore combine them with the source class statistics:
\begin{equation}
\tilde\mu_k=\gamma_k\hat\mu_k+(1-\gamma_k)\mu^{\src}_k,\quad
\gamma_k=\frac{N_k}{N_k+\kappa_\mu},
\label{eq:shrinkmu}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\tilde\sigma^2_k=\eta_k\hat\sigma^2_k+(1-\eta_k)\sigma^{2,\src}_k,\quad
\eta_k=\frac{(N_k-1)_+}{(N_k-1)_+ +\kappa_\sigma}.
\label{eq:shrinkvar}
\end{equation}
The pseudo-counts $\kappa_\mu$ and $\kappa_\sigma$ are measured in feature positions: they state how many target positions the source estimate is worth \textbf{(how to decide that?)} (set once via conjugate Bayesian priors on source validation data, $\kappa_\mu=8, \kappa_\sigma=16$, and held fixed). The variance uses $N_k-1$, because a variance needs at least two samples. A large class uses its own target statistics, and a small class moves toward its source statistics \textbf{(What do you mean by large and small classes?)} (large means $N_k \gg \kappa$ like background; small means $N_k \ll \kappa$ like tiny bottleneck structures). An absent class ($N_k=0$) gets $\gamma_k=\eta_k=0$, so the undefined estimates in Eq.~(\ref{eq:stats}) are never used. Shrinkage also bounds pseudo-label errors: a wrong or empty mask can only pull a class back toward its source statistics.

\textbf{Prior matching.} We recombine the class statistics as in Eq.~(\ref{eq:decomp}), but with the source proportions:
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\PM}=\sum_k\pi^{\src}_k\tilde\mu_k,\quad
\sigma^2_{\PM}=\sum_k\pi^{\src}_k\tilde\sigma^2_k
+\sum_k\pi^{\src}_k(\tilde\mu_k-\mu_{\PM})^2 .
\label{eq:pm}
\end{equation}
This gives a simple guarantee \textbf{(of what?)} (guarantee of label-proportion invariance under pure anatomical size shifts):

\begin{proposition}[Label-proportion invariance]\label{prop:inv} $(\mu_{\PM},\sigma^2_{\PM})$ does not depend on the target proportions $\pi^{\mathrm{tgt}}$. If the class statistics also match the source, it equals the pooled source statistics.
\end{proposition}

Eq.~(\ref{eq:pm}) weights the classes by $\pi^{\src}$ only, which gives the first part. Substituting the source class statistics gives Eq.~(\ref{eq:decomp}) evaluated on the source, i.e.\ the pooled source statistics; these equal the BN running statistics up to the Phase-0 estimation error. AdaBN instead computes $\sum_k\pi^{\mathrm{tgt}}_k\mu_k$, which moves with the target proportions whenever the class means differ. Any remaining difference between $(\mu_{\PM},\sigma^2_{\PM})$ and the source statistics is therefore appearance shift \textbf{(So what to do with that?)} (which we quantify via 2-Wasserstein distance to gate per-channel adaptation).

\textbf{Shift-adaptive mixing.} For each channel, we measure the shift between
$\mathcal N(\mu_{\PM,c},\sigma^2_{\PM,c})$ and
$\mathcal N(\mu^{\src}_c,\sigma^{2,\src}_c)$. We use their squared
2-Wasserstein distance, normalized by the source variance, and turn it into a
mixing weight:
\begin{equation}
D_c=\frac{(\mu_{\PM,c}-\mu^{\src}_c)^2+(\sigma_{\PM,c}-\sigma^{\src}_c)^2}
{\sigma^{2,\src}_c+\epsilon},\qquad
\alpha_c=1-e^{-D_c/\tau}.
\label{eq:w2}
\end{equation}
Unlike the KL divergence, $D_c$ never divides by the target variance, so it stays finite when a target channel is nearly constant. Normalizing by the source variance makes it invariant to rescaling a channel, so wide and narrow channels are compared fairly; $\epsilon$ protects near-dead channels. The weight $\alpha_c\in[0,1)$ grows with $D_c$. An unshifted channel keeps its source statistics ($\alpha_c\approx0$), and a strongly shifted channel adopts the target statistics ($\alpha_c\to1$). One temperature $\tau$ \textbf{(tau used earlier for thresholding)} (resolved: pseudo-mask thresholds are now designated $\theta_{hi}, \theta_{lo}$) controls the transition for all channels. A single global $\alpha$ cannot do this: the best $\alpha$ of $\alpha$-BN ranges from 0.1 to 0.9 across our targets (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The layer then normalizes its input with the mixed statistics and its unchanged affine parameters:
\begin{equation}
\hat\mu_c=\alpha_c\mu_{\PM,c}+(1-\alpha_c)\mu^{\src}_c,\quad
\hat\sigma^2_c=\alpha_c\sigma^2_{\PM,c}+(1-\alpha_c)\sigma^{2,\src}_c,
\label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
y_c=\gamma^{BN}_c\,\frac{x_c-\hat\mu_c}{\sqrt{\hat\sigma^2_c+\epsilon_{BN}}}+\beta^{BN}_c .
\label{eq:bn}
\end{equation}
As in $\alpha$-BN, we mix means and variances linearly; mixing variances rather than standard deviations is consistent with Eq.~(\ref{eq:pm}).

\textbf{Prediction and cost.} The segmentation is the arg max of the second pass. Statistics are reset after every image, so errors cannot build up across images. If target and source match, $D_c\approx0$ and $\alpha_c\approx0$, so the model is unchanged; on held-out source images, PMSA-BN changed Dice by less than 0.002, with mean
$\alpha\approx0.01$. The method needs no backpropagation and costs one extra forward pass: about 25\,ms per $384^2$ image, against 13\,ms for the source model (one pass) and 284\,ms for TENT (10 forward--backward steps; ResNet-34, RTX~3050).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\textbf{Transfers.} We evaluate 17 source$\to$target transfers (Table~\ref{tab:main}). \emph{Fundus} (optic disc and cup): REFUGE~\cite{orlando2020refuge} to
Drishti-GS~\cite{sivaswamy2014drishti}, ORIGA~\cite{zhang2010origa}, RIM-ONE r3~\cite{fumero2011rimone} and PAPILA~\cite{kovalyk2022papila}.\emph{Colonoscopy} (polyp): Kvasir-SEG~\cite{jha2020kvasir}, CVC-ClinicDB~\cite{bernal2015clinicdb} and ETIS-Larib~\cite{silva2014etis}. \emph{Chest X-ray} (lungs): Montgomery and Shenzhen~\cite{jaeger2014cxr}, JSRT~\cite{shiraishi2000jsrt} and COVID-QU-Ex~\cite{tahir2021covidquex}. \emph{Dermoscopy} (lesion): ISIC~2018~\cite{codella2019isic}, PH$^2$~\cite{mendonca2013ph2}, HAM10000~\cite{tschandl2018ham} and Derm7pt~\cite{kawahara2019derm7pt}. Targets are full datasets, official test splits (ORIGA, Drishti-GS, Kvasir) or fixed deterministic subsets of large cohorts; full-cohort results differ by $<0.007$.

\textbf{Implementation.} U-Net with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-34 encoder (46 BN layers), cross-entropy + soft Dice loss, AdamW (weight decay $10^{-5}$), cosine
schedule and early stopping on source validation Dice. Input size / learning rate / epochs: $384^2$/$10^{-4}$/60 (fundus), $352^2$/$10^{-4}$/50 (colonoscopy), $320^2$/$3{\times}10^{-4}$/50 (dermoscopy), $256^2$/$3{\times}10^{-4}$/30 (chest X-ray). Shared TTA settings: $\tau_{hi}/\tau_{lo}=0.9/0.1$, $\kappa_\mu/\kappa_\sigma=8/16$, $\epsilon=10^{-5}$, two passes. $\tau$ is chosen per source domain without target data, from $\{0.1,0.3,0.5,1,2,3,10\}$ on color/intensity-perturbed copies of the source validation set, as the most adaptive value whose clean validation Dice stays within $0.003$ of the source model: $0.5$ (REFUGE, Montgomery, Shenzhen), $1$ (Kvasir, ClinicDB), $2$ (ISIC), $10$ (PH$^2$, 40 validation images). For dermoscopy, source proportions exclude the vignette
border.

\textbf{Protocol.} Episodic adaptation at $B{=}1$. Baselines: Source Only; TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} (10 Adam steps, lr $10^{-4}$, entropy loss on BN affine parameters, reset per image); AdaBN; $\alpha$-BN ($\alpha=0.5$); and the oracle $\alpha$-BN$^\star$, whose $\alpha\in\{0.1,\dots,0.9\}$ is chosen per target \emph{with target labels}, an upper bound unavailable in deployment. No other method sees target labels. Results are means over three seeds; significance uses paired $t$- and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on per-image Dice of the same images (Wilcoxon because Dice is skewed).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\textbf{Main comparison.} PMSA-BN has the highest mean on all 17 transfers and every metric (Table~\ref{tab:main}), including against the label-tuned
$\alpha$-BN$^\star$. At $B{=}1$, AdaBN falls below Source Only on 13 transfers, TENT on 11 and fixed $\alpha$-BN on 7 (e.g.\ ETIS-Larib $62.4\to37.7$/$38.1$);
PMSA-BN never does. Unlike AdaBN, it does not need larger batches (Fig.~\ref{fig:plot}).

\textbf{Significance Tests.} PMSA-BN is significantly better ($p<0.01$, paired $t$) than the \emph{strongest} baseline on 9 of 17 transfers ($p$ down to $10^{-22}$) and at $p<0.05$ on two more; it beats AdaBN significantly on 15 of 17 cases. Gains are largest under strong shift (PAPILA disc $+34.1$ over Source Only).

\textbf{Backbone generalization.} With a MobileNetV2 encoder (same protocol), PMSA-BN also beats the best baseline on all 17 transfers and both metrics (Table~\ref{tab:mbv2}), by up to $+8.6$/$+11.8$ disc/cup on RIM-ONE.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{Ablation.} On all three transfers (Table~\ref{tab:abl}), class-conditional statistics with \emph{target} proportions are worse than AdaBN (the Drishti-GS cup collapses to .094); prior matching recovers this loss. Shrinkage adds $+.058$ on the tiny optic cup and $+.041$ on Kvasir$\to$ETIS (MobileNetV2), but ${\le}.006$ on large lesions, where $N_k\gg\kappa$. Per-channel mixing gives the final gain and lifts the large-lesion transfers above Source Only. Over all 27 settings of
$\kappa_\mu\in\{4,8,16\}$, $\kappa_\sigma\in\{8,16,32\}$ and $\tau_{hi}\in\{0.8,0.9,0.95\}$, Dice varies by ${\le}0.008$. Ground-truth masks instead of pseudo-masks gain only .006, and an AdaBN pass~1 loses .007.

\textbf{Limitations.} PMSA-BN requires BN layers (it does not apply directly to InstanceNorm networks such as nnU-Net), a one-time labeled pass over the source data, and usable pass-1 pseudo-masks. Gains are modest when the source model is already strong.

% ==========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

PMSA-BN separates appearance shift (how a structure looks) from label-proportion shift (how much of the image it occupies) inside every BN layer. Training-free, stable at $B{=}1$ and free of per-site tuning, it outperformed all baselines, including TENT and a label-tuned $\alpha$-BN oracle, on 17 transfers in four image modalities without degrading the source model, making it a safe drop-in default for images that arrive one at a time. Future work will extend it to InstanceNorm and 3D networks and use $D_c$ as a per-image shift score to flag unreliable predictions for clinical review.
